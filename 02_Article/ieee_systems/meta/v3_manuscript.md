## ABSTRACT (replaces paragraph #7)

Abstract—Wildfire response is a system-of-systems problem: data providers, a hazard model, decision agents, and an incident commander must act together, under time pressure, on a partly observed fire. Existing decision support systems choose from closed catalogues of predefined actions, cannot revise their logic during an incident, and rarely expose their reasoning. DisasterAware is a distributed decision support architecture that separates a simulation core from a decision core reading the hazard state only through confidence-rated observations. Per region a local agent maps ten observation features onto five decision concepts and, through a sparse Takagi-Sugeno rule base, onto six intervention families, so every order is readable by an officer; a non-inferential coordination layer ranks regions and shares capacity. The central contribution is the open intervention space. When a decision falls short, a staged loop tunes rules, writes rules for uncovered situations, and lets a proposer compose new interventions from seven physical effects the simulator implements, placed on named sectors of the fire. Every proposal must clear deterministic gates and a paired forecast test on two reseeded futures before it acts, so doctrine grows only as the incident demands, life-safety orders are never attenuated, and every order stays traceable; the commander keeps a tool that still answers when the fire leaves the playbook. With three library tactics withheld from the doctrine, the loop re-composed an equivalent of at least one of them from the effect primitives in {{E1.any_share_evo_pct}} to {{E1.any_share_rnd_pct}} percent of the incidents, and with the library available the open space reduced burned area by {{E2.open_vs_closed_pct}} percent against the same adaptive rule base over eight scenario families, on every family and with the largest gains where the doctrine is structurally short. An evolutionary search and a random sampler were admitted through the same gates at a similar rate and reached similar outcomes, which shows that the guarantees rest on the verifier and not on the proposer.

Index Terms—System-of-systems architecture, distributed decision support, open intervention space, fuzzy concept-based reasoning, generative rule proposal, verification and fail-safe design, wildfire emergency management.

## I. INTRODUCTION (paragraphs #10-#12 kept; #22-#26 replaced by the following)

DisasterAware answers these gaps with an architecture whose central contribution is the open intervention space: the set of orders the system may issue is not fixed in advance but grows during the incident, under verification by the physical model. This article asks three questions about that space. Can a decision layer compose, from the physical effects a simulator can execute, interventions that its doctrine does not contain, and can it do so where the doctrine is structurally insufficient rather than merely untuned? Does the safety of the result depend on who proposes, or on what verifies? And what does the layer learn from the simulator about which compositions help? Three contributions follow, matched to the gaps of Table I.

Open intervention space over a verified basis. To address G1, the fixed menu of alternatives becomes an open composition over a closed and verified basis. Ten observation features aggregate into five decision concepts, so rules are written in operational language, and the field of orders is reshaped online on three levels: an evolving Takagi-Sugeno base adapts memberships and consequents [26, 27], term resolution grows where situations are ambiguous, and a generative stage may add intermediate concepts, bundle existing families, and compose seven verified physical effects into sited interventions that carry their own geometry. The proposer behind the generative stage is interchangeable; two search proposers are compared here and a language-model proposer is carried through the same interface. Nothing enters the base without a gate chain that ends in a paired forecast on two independently seeded futures [28].

Rediscovery as the test of openness. To address G2, the claim that the space is open is tested against a reference that is not the system itself. Three tactics of the written doctrine are withheld from the vocabulary, the brief, the schema, and the allocator, leaving only the effect primitives, and the campaign measures whether the loop writes an equivalent of each on its own, how soon, and what it buys. Explanation remains structural, since every admitted object is logged with the deficit that triggered it, the gate that judged it, and the rule that consumes it.

End-to-end measurement over scenario families built for the question. To address G3, the two cores are strictly separated and validated independently, and the decision layer is measured on eight scenario families, five from earlier work and three in which the closed doctrine cannot win by tuning: a lakeside fire where only drafting adds capacity, a fast interface fire where only coating prevents ignition in time, and a long incident on a spent pool. Paired with and without runs share world, ignitions, and random stream, so differences arise from the issued orders alone.

This work is a systems contribution rather than a new inference algorithm. It defines the open intervention space as an architectural object, namely the set of orders the system may issue at a given time together with the grammar and the gates under which that set may be extended, states the invariants this object preserves, proves them for the acceptance and fail-safe gates of Section IV-H, and measures the resulting architecture end to end. The concept-based rule layer and the proposers are components inside the architecture; either can be replaced without affecting the invariants, and the proposer comparison of Section VII-C shows this empirically.

## II. RELATED WORK AND BACKGROUND (heading replaces "II. Background"; II.A kept; II.B replaced by B, C, D below; old II.C, II.D, II.E become E, F, G with their text unchanged)

### B. Closed and Open Decision Spaces

Decision analysis has long assumed that the set of alternatives is given before evaluation begins. Arbel and Tong [35] identified options generation as a neglected phase that precedes choice resolution and proposed an interactive aid in which hierarchical prioritization and sensitivity analysis prompt the decision maker to enlarge the option set, while leaving generation itself to the human. Colorni and Tsoukias [36, 37] later formalized the construction of the alternative set as a recursion of decision problems, distinguished known, partially known, and unknown decision spaces, and argued that unknown alternatives arise only through backtracking and through revision of the separable dimensions that describe the set. Hofstede [38] placed the same distinction at the level of system design: a planner faces an open problem whose boundaries are decided at planning time, whereas a developer builds a formal problem with no loose ends, and planning systems that hard-wire the open aspects are not adopted. Cognitive evidence points the same way. Del Missier et al. [39] show that option generation in ill-structured problems depends on ideation beyond memory retrieval, that individuals cover only part of the available option space, and that generating more options raises the quality of the best option while lowering the mean quality of the set. Together these works motivate a decision support system that generates alternatives during operation, and they also set the condition under which such generation is safe: broad generation must be paired with strict, external evaluation.

### C. Mechanisms That Open a Decision Space

Three families of mechanisms have been used to open a decision space. The first searches a fixed parameter space for structurally different solutions. Fazlollahi and Vahidov [40] argued that an active DSS should generate diverse alternatives and keep their generation separate from their evaluation, and operationalized this with a genetic algorithm whose fuzzy fitness was computed through a Monte Carlo business simulator; modeling to generate alternatives follows the same idea in mathematical programming [41]. The second composes alternatives from modules. Levin [42] characterizes the shift from choosing among given alternatives to synthesizing composite alternatives, in which a solution is assembled from design alternatives for each component under compatibility constraints, and Struss [43] realizes therapy proposal as model composition over a library of processes whose causal completion is bounded by action triggers, with plan evaluation kept as a separate, prediction-based step. The third extends the action model itself at run time, as in open-world planning and action discovery [44, 45]. In all three families the space that is opened remains bounded by a basis the system already understands: a decision vector, a module library, or a set of action schemas. The open space is an open composition over a closed and verified basis, and this is the sense in which the present work uses the term. Two consequences follow for the design. Generation must be cheap to reject, because most of the composition space leaves the outcome unchanged, and the evaluator must be independent of the generator, because a generator that grades itself cannot support a safety claim.

### D. Wildfire and Disaster Decision Support With Learned and Generative Components

Recent wildfire and disaster systems have added learned and generative components, but the intervention space has stayed closed or unchecked. Dubey and Dubey [12] couple a Transformer forecaster with a Mamdani rule base of fifteen expert-defined rules that maps predicted risk to four fixed response levels, and they name the handcrafted rule base as a limitation under unusual conditions. Chandra et al. [46] combine an ontology of 110 SWRL rules with stream processing and a retrieval-based language model, so that suggested actions are drawn from a predefined rule base and checked only for consistency with the ontology. Cheng et al. [47] use language models to assign AHP weights over a fixed hierarchy of sixteen indicators and verify them only by correlation with a single expert table. Gerolimos et al. [48] argue for open world machine learning in disaster management, but the openness concerns the recognition of previously unseen event classes, while the response stage remains an unspecified mapping to familiar actions. At the other extreme, Sandeep et al. [49] and Tan [50] let multi-agent language models produce free-text response plans; hallucination is contained by retrieval grounding, output schemas, self-correction, or a post-hoc statistical grading of descriptive claims, and plan quality is assessed qualitatively or after generation. Cui et al. [51] survey this landscape and conclude that hallucination risk calls for external verification through physics-based constraints and deterministic simulation, yet their own decision-support case confines the agent to a fixed library of engineering tools. Simulator-centred systems are the closest neighbours. WildfireKR [52] uses a language-model agent with schema-constrained tool calls to automate calibrated cellular-automaton spread simulation, reducing scenario setup from hours to minutes, but it produces suppression-free envelopes and leaves intervention design to the analyst. The gap that remains is a system in which the intervention space is open at run time, every new intervention is built from effects a physical model can execute, and admission is decided by the physical model rather than by the proposer, by a schema, or by the decision maker alone. DisasterAware fills this gap, and Section VII tests it against a reference outside the system: the doctrine it was not given.

## V. THE OPEN INTERVENTION SPACE (new Heading1, inserted before the old "V. Results"; old V becomes VII after the new VI)

### A. Levels of Novelty and the Grammar

What counts as a new intervention is stated before it is measured. Four levels are distinguished. At level zero the layer changes the intensity of one of the six families; this is the closed space of the deployed tools. At level one it bundles existing families at fixed weights, a composite alternative in the sense of [42]; a bundle is a convenience and is not counted as new. At level two it writes a sited intervention: a named list of at most three clauses, each applying one of seven physical effects (wet, clear, ignite, coat, evacuate, prime, draft) to one of seven sectors of the incident (head, flank, rear, ring, at-fire, assets, populated) over a distance band in cells with an amount. The band is measured from the sector's reference cells, the burning cells for the first five sectors, the threatened asset footprints, or the populated cells, and a band starting at zero includes the reference cells themselves, so two clauses that differ in range never compile to the same cells. A sited intervention states where and how, in terms no base family expresses; it is the object the claim of this article rests on. At level three the layer adds an intermediate concept, a normalized weighted sum of at most four existing features or concepts, which adds an antecedent axis to the space and corresponds to the expansion of separable dimensions in [36]. The seven effects are the fields the engine already writes and the cost model already prices; the generative stage never defines a new physical effect. Three library tactics are built on the same effects and validated in the engine, tactical burn (ignite on the band ahead of the head), retardant drop (coat on the band ahead of the head), and water drafting (draft on committed cells), and they share their physics with the corresponding clause effects through common operators, so a clause that re-composes a library tactic is measured on the same physics as the tactic itself.

### B. Staged Adaptation

Adaptation is engaged only when the standing decision demonstrably falls short: a cost deficit, a coverage gap while fire is alive, or renewed spread under applied orders. Stage 1 tunes rules already in the base with two bounded moves: the consequent step ( 23 ) nudges the intensity of a rule by a fixed 0.05 within the unit interval, and the boundary shift moves the shared edge of two neighboring terms while preserving the partition of unity. Stage 2 grows the base: it writes a rule on the uncovered combination of the two most active concepts, and where a covered combination is ambiguous it inserts a narrow term at the crisp reading. Stage 3 asks a proposer for a rule the current vocabulary cannot express, alone or as a package with exactly one new object of level one, two, or three that the rule must use. Which stage runs in a cycle is chosen by a contextual bandit over the deficit band and the coverage flag [32]; a stage that keeps failing loses value in its own table. Every trial of every stage is judged on a working copy of the base by the same quantity, the forecast physical cost on the shadow simulator over a basis of at least 45 minutes, and is kept only when that cost falls ( 24 ).

### C. Proposers and the Trial Context

The generative stage is a function from a situation to a candidate, and nothing that decides admission depends on which function is used. Three proposers are placed behind the same hook. The language-model proposer sends a standing mission brief and the situation of the cycle to a pretrained model used in context only, with a fixed reply schema and a revision budget of two corrected retries. The evolutionary proposer searches the grammar of Section V-A directly: a population of twelve candidates, three generations, tournament selection, mutation of effects, sectors, ranges, amounts, terms, and intensities, and crossover by clause exchange. The random proposer samples the grammar uniformly. Both search proposers apply the cheap gates of Section V-D as a filter before any forecast is spent, price each surviving candidate by the gain of one seeded shadow forecast, re-price their finalists on the second seed, and return the candidate with the better worse-seed gain; when no candidate lowers the forecast on both futures they return nothing and the stage records a search miss without spending the four gate forecasts. Every proposer is charged the same currency, the number of shadow forecasts it spends per cycle, capped at 36 for the search proposers, so the comparison of Section VII-C is at equal budget. The stage leaves a trial context on the engine for the duration of the call, the current base, the gated activations, and the build function the gates themselves use, so a search proposer prices candidates exactly as the gates will; it cannot admit anything.

### D. Verification Gates

A proposal is rejected the moment it fails a gate that applies to it. G1 checks form and G2 arity, vocabulary, and range. G2c requires every antecedent to fire in the present situation; a term that fails only by its form is repaired to the nearest reading that still holds and the repair is recorded. G2 availability refuses water-dependent effects on a map without water, G2d refuses capacity-spending orders on a saturated pool, and G2b refuses a duplicate antecedent cell, a redundant concept (cosine above 0.95 with an existing one), or a clause signature already admitted. None of these costs a simulation. G3 and G4 are one paired comparison on two independently seeded futures: the base extended by the candidate against the base without it, and the forecast physical cost must fall on both ( 25 ). G5 asks a package to clear a vocabulary margin on both futures, and G5b, when the new object is ordered beside ordinary interventions, repeats the two futures with the object struck out and admits the package only if the cost rises without it, so the vocabulary does not grow on borrowed credit. The gates contain no model. They are the same for the language model, the search, and the random sampler, which is what makes the proposer comparison meaningful.

### E. Learning From the Simulator

What the layer learns is kept in three places. The lineage store holds every admitted rule and object, so a later rule in the same incident may order a generated intervention or cite a generated concept, and a later incident restores them unless the vocabulary is reset. The cycle chronicle holds every closed stage-3 attempt with its situation, proposal, gate, and measured change, and the nearest past cases are placed in the next proposal request. The composition prior is a table over context buckets (pool saturated or not, water present or not, assets within reach of the fire or not) and effect-sector pairs, holding the number of trials, the number of positive forecast gains, and the mean gain; it is updated from every priced candidate and every gate verdict, it seeds half of the evolutionary population, and it is the measurable form of the statement that the decision layer learns from the simulator which compositions of its physical primitives help. Section VII-C switches it off to measure what it adds.

## VI. EVALUATION DESIGN (new Heading1; takes old V.A criteria table as A and old V.B hindcast as B unchanged; C, D new)

### C. Scenario Families

Eight scenario families are used, all on 72 by 50 cell grids at 30 m with a two-minute step, four regions, a twelve-minute decision cycle, a 24-minute planning horizon, a satisficing bound of 0.35, a quality gate of 0.60, the five-rule seed profile, one adaptation window per cycle, and episodes to extinction or a twelve-hour cap (eighteen hours on S8). Table V defines them. S1 to S5 are the families of the earlier ladder campaign: remote forest and wildland-urban interface, each with a sufficient and a limited pool, and the interface case under degraded observation. S6 to S8 are built so that the closed doctrine is structurally insufficient rather than merely untuned. In S6 a lake lies eight to eleven cells downwind of the first ignition and the pool is at 40 percent, so the only way to add capacity is to draft from the water; in S7 the interface burns under 12 m/s wind and dry fuel from two ignitions near twelve settlements, so protection that raises a defensive posture cannot prevent ignition while coating ahead of the head can; in S8 three remote ignitions run for up to eighteen hours on a 40 percent pool, so the capacity-spending effects are rationed away and only drafting or counter-fire remain. The coordinator's doctrine-free drafting directive is switched off in every arm, so a tactic reaches the field only through a rule.

### D. Arms, Experiments, and Measures

Five arms share every world: T0 issues no orders; F5 runs the five seed rules without adaptation; F5+Ev adds stages 1 and 2 and is the closed reference, since it may tune and grow the rule base but never the vocabulary; Open/Evo and Open/Random add stage 3 with the evolutionary and the random proposer at the same budget of 36 shadow forecasts per cycle. The language-model proposer is implemented behind the same hook and schema, but its arm needs an interactive model session per run and is not part of the campaign reported here; the comparison therefore concerns the two reproducible search proposers. Four experiments follow. E1 withholds tactical burn, retardant drop, and water drafting together from every arm on S6 to S8 and measures rediscovery: a run rediscovers a tactic when it admits a sited intervention whose effect matches it (draft on a fire-referenced sector within four cells, coat on head or ring within six cells, ignite on head, ring, or flank within eight cells); the share of runs, the minute of first admission, and the paired burned area against F5+Ev are reported over 30 seeds. E2 runs the five arms on all eight families with the full library available, {{E2.seeds}} seeds each, and reports outcome, cost, and the paired difference against the closed reference. E3 compares the two search proposers with and without the composition prior on S6 to S8 at an equal forecast budget, 30 seeds each, by attempts, admissions, forecasts per admission, object kinds, and outcome. E4 sweeps the vocabulary margin of G5 over three decades, 10^-4 to 10^-2, on the same families with the evolutionary proposer, {{E4.seeds}} seeds per margin. Every stage-3 attempt is written with its proposer, kind, gate, and measured futures, and every admitted object with its clauses and the minute of admission.

### G. Compliance With the Evaluation Criteria

Predictive fidelity rests on four blind hindcasts on real inputs, with front agreement of 86 to 94 percent and detection probability of 85 to 97 percent. Decision quality is met against the closed adaptive reference on all eight families, with paired gains in burned area and a higher share of incidents brought to extinction. Adaptability is met on the axis the literature names, rediscovery of withheld knowledge, with {{E1.any_share_evo_pct}} to {{E1.any_share_rnd_pct}} percent of incidents re-composing a withheld tactic. Timeliness and scalability carry over from the earlier measurements and hold with the search budget added. Robustness is shown for observation loss on S5 and not for sensor noise or communication failure. Explainability holds at the level of the ledger: every attempt, verdict, and object is logged with its trigger, gate, and consuming rule; interpretability to the operator is not evaluated. Uncertainty is carried into every decision as a confidence weight and is not propagated as an ensemble.

## VIII. DISCUSSION: SYSTEMS LESSONS AND LIMITATIONS (replaces old VI)

Three lessons transfer beyond wildfire. First, openness is a property of the verifier, not of the generator. Two proposers of very different character, one that searches and one that merely samples the grammar, were admitted through the same gates at a similar rate, reached similar outcomes, and never fielded an order the forecast judged harmful; what the proposer changes is how many forecasts an admission costs and which objects are found first. A system that lets its generator grade its own output cannot make this statement, and most of the systems reviewed in Section II-D cannot. Second, the claim that a space is open needs a reference outside the system. Rediscovery of withheld doctrine supplies one: the tactics the layer re-composed were written by fire officers, not by the authors, and the layer was given only the effects beneath them. Third, the value of an open space lies in geometry as much as in vocabulary. The closed adaptation, which tunes and writes rules over region-wide intensities, moved the outcome by a few hectares; the sited intervention, which puts an effect on a sector of the fire at a range, moved it by a third to a half on every family, including those where the doctrine already held the fire. A benchmark that wants to separate the two effects needs families in which the doctrine lacks the tactic itself, and the three built here are what make the rediscovery claim measurable.

Three limitations bound the claims. The evaluation is entirely simulated; the hazard model is validated on real records, but no order was executed in a real incident and no fire officer took part in the loop, and the life-safety channels were rarely exercised because the scenario families place the fire away from the settlements for most of the episode. The open space is bounded by its basis of seven effects and seven sectors; it cannot discover a new physical effect, and a composition the engine cannot price returns a forecast identical to the baseline and is rejected for that reason. Finally, the search proposers are reproducible from their seeds, whereas the language-model proposer is not bitwise reproducible and was not benchmarked in this campaign; it is carried through the same hook, schema, and gates, so no admission can depend on it, and its comparison is left to a run with an interactive model session.

## IX. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK (replaces old VII)

This article presented DisasterAware, a distributed decision support architecture for wildfire response built on two separated cores, and asked whether its decision layer can compose, from the physical effects a simulator can execute, interventions its doctrine does not contain. It can, under three conditions that the architecture enforces: the objects are built from a verified basis, the admission test is a paired forecast on two reseeded futures that no proposer can influence, and the loop is given situations in which the doctrine is structurally short. With three library tactics withheld, the layer re-composed an equivalent of at least one in about half of the incidents and reduced burned area against the closed adaptive reference on all three families built for the test; with the library available, the open space reduced burned area by {{E2.open_vs_closed_pct}} percent over eight families and on every one of them, while the closed adaptation alone moved it by a few hectares. Two search proposers were compared at equal budget and admitted through the same gates at a similar rate, and a learned composition prior steered the evolutionary search towards sited interventions and lowered the cost of an admission without changing what the gates let through. Four studies follow. A tabletop exercise will place fire officers in the loop with and without the framework and will put the generated objects, with their names and rationales, in front of the people who would have to order them. A transfer study will keep the decision layer and the grammar and replace the simulation core with another spreading hazard, the rewriting cost separating what is architecture from what is wildfire. Inside the simulator, an ensemble study will test the stability of admissions across plausible forecasts, and a scenario study will place population in the path of the fire so that the life-safety channels and the evacuation effects of the grammar are exercised as hard as the suppression effects were here.
