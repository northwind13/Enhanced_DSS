Operational assessments are graded and linguistic, such as a high fire threat or a low suppression feasibility. Fuzzy set theory represents such judgments as partial membership in a linguistic term and lets domain knowledge be written as IF-THEN rules over these terms {c32}, so a graded observation becomes a decision without a crisp threshold. The Takagi-Sugeno (TS) form is chosen because its consequents are constants, so the output is a firing-strength weighted average without centroid integration {c33}. This gives a numeric intensity command that the simulator can apply, and it lets rules be tuned online with a predictable effect, as evolving TS models have shown {c34}. Rules cannot be written over the features directly. With {m:N} features and {m:T} terms per feature a complete rule base needs {m:T^N} antecedents, which for ten features and five terms is {m:5^10}, nearly ten million. The remedy is a conceptual middle layer. Features are composed into decision concepts by weighted aggregation, and rules are written over the concepts only, so the antecedent space falls to {m:5^5 = 3125}. VODA uses five concepts because each answers one of the five questions that operators ask during response, as documented in fire management and naturalistic decision research {c3;23}, namely how severe the fire threat is, whether an intervention can feasibly be mounted, how exposed the assets are, how urgent action is, and whether evacuation pressure is building. The concepts are fire threat level, suppression feasibility, asset exposure risk, intervention urgency, and evacuation pressure. Each answers its question completely and no other, and a rule over them reads as an operational statement, which is what makes every recommendation explainable.
