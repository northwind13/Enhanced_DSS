Operational assessments arrive as graded, linguistic judgments, such as a high fire threat or a low suppression feasibility. Fuzzy set theory gives such judgments a formal counterpart, partial membership in a linguistic term, and lets domain knowledge be written as IF-THEN rules over these terms {c32}. Fuzzy inference is therefore the natural way to turn graded observations into a decision without forcing crisp thresholds. Among the inference forms, the Takagi-Sugeno (TS) form is chosen because its consequents are constants rather than fuzzy sets, so the output is a firing-strength weighted average that needs no centroid integration {c33}. This matters here for two reasons. The output must be a numeric intensity command that the simulator can apply, and the rules must be tunable online with a predictable effect, which evolving TS models have shown within a fixed vocabulary {c34}. Rules cannot be written over the features directly. With {m:N} features and {m:T} linguistic terms per feature, a complete rule base needs {m:T^N} antecedents. For the ten features and five terms used here this is {m:5^10}, nearly ten million combinations, which can neither be elicited from experts nor audited by operators. The remedy is a conceptual middle layer. Features are first composed into a small number of decision concepts by weighted aggregation, and rules are written over the concepts only, so the antecedent space falls to {m:5^5 = 3125} combinations, of which only the active subset is instantiated. The concepts are not chosen freely. Each one answers exactly one question that operators ask during response {c3;23}, namely how severe the fire threat is, whether an intervention can feasibly be mounted, how exposed the assets are, how urgent action is, and whether evacuation pressure is building. These five questions yield the five decision concepts, fire threat level, suppression feasibility, asset exposure risk, intervention urgency, and evacuation pressure. Each concept answers its question completely and no other, so the set is free of redundancy, and a rule over the concepts reads as an operational statement, which is what makes every recommendation explainable.
